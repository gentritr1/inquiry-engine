# Discovery Engine

> **Purpose:** A reusable research methodology for discovering, testing,
> refining, and applying theories across any domain while minimizing
> elegant but false explanations.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Core Philosophy

The goal is **not** to generate interesting ideas.

The goal is to generate **better approximations of reality**.

Every theory must earn trust through:

1.  Compression
2.  Prediction
3.  Falsification
4.  Revision

Reality always has veto power.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Constitution

-   Reality outranks elegance.
-   Compression without prediction is philosophy.
-   Prediction without mechanism is correlation.
-   Mechanism without evidence is speculation.
-   Every theory must compete against alternatives.
-   Unknowns are first-class outputs.
-   Confidence is calibrated to evidence.
-   Beautiful explanations are guilty until proven innocent.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 1 --- Observe

Collect observations only.

Separate:

-   Facts
-   Measurements
-   Anecdotes
-   Assumptions
-   Unknowns

Never explain yet.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 2 --- Evidence Ledger

Grade every claim:

A --- Direct evidence

B --- Strong inference

C --- Weak inference

D --- Unknown

E --- Contradicted

No borrowing strength across grades.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 3 --- Ontology

Before solving anything ask:

What actually exists?

Identify:

-   Entities
-   Processes
-   Relationships
-   Constraints
-   Information
-   Energy
-   Time
-   Feedback loops

Many failures originate here.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 4 --- Hidden Assumptions

List assumptions.

Especially:

-   constitutive assumptions
-   economic assumptions
-   physical assumptions
-   psychological assumptions
-   historical assumptions
-   linguistic assumptions

Try removing each.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 5 --- Residual Search

Find where current theories fail.

Search for:

-   anomalies
-   unexplained observations
-   stable errors
-   contradictions
-   edge cases

Residuals receive highest attention.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 6 --- Representation

Attempt different representations.

Examples:

Graph

Network

Field

Agent

Information

Optimization

Game

Constraint

Search

Evolution

Many discoveries happen here.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 7 --- Candidate Theories

Always build multiple competing theories.

At minimum:

-   technological
-   economic
-   evolutionary
-   institutional
-   psychological
-   energetic

Compare instead of committing.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 8 --- Mechanism Gate

Reject any explanation lacking a mechanism.

Pattern alone is insufficient.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 9 --- Constraint Pass

Check against:

-   physics
-   thermodynamics
-   incentives
-   economics
-   computation
-   politics
-   scalability
-   human behavior

Reject impossible theories.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 10 --- Predictions

Every theory must produce:

-   1-year prediction
-   3-year prediction
-   5-year prediction

Each prediction must be:

Specific

Observable

Falsifiable

Dated

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 11 --- Adversarial Review

Attack your own theory.

Search harder against it than for it.

Ask:

What kills it?

Who disagrees?

What assumptions fail?

What evidence changes my mind?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Phase 12 --- Reality Review

Update confidence.

Record:

Confirmed

Modified

Rejected

Unknown

Never defend sunk costs.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Universal Heuristics

1.  Hunt residuals.
2.  Challenge constitutive assumptions.
3.  Demand mechanisms.
4.  Change representation.
5.  Import ideas across domains carefully.
6.  Prefer natural experiments.
7.  Compare rival explanations.
8.  Keep confidence calibrated.
9.  Prefer prediction over elegance.
10. Revise continuously.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Validation Suite

## Test 1 --- Historical Rediscovery

Goal: Reconstruct discoveries using only pre-discovery evidence.

Suggested cases:

-   Darwin (1858 evidence)
-   Einstein (1904 evidence)
-   Shannon (1947 evidence)
-   Wegener (1912 evidence)
-   Turing (1935 evidence)
-   Semmelweis (1847 evidence)
-   Marshall & H. pylori
-   Plate tectonics

Metrics:

-   anomaly identified
-   assumptions challenged
-   mechanism proposed
-   prediction quality
-   similarity to historical breakthrough

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Test 2 --- Cross-Domain Generality

Run identical process on:

Physics

Biology

Economics

Software

Organizations

Psychology

Language

Pass criterion:

Method remains useful without changing structure.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Test 3 --- Prediction

Generate dated predictions.

Review every:

6 months

12 months

36 months

Score:

Correct

Partially correct

Wrong

Unknown

Update confidence.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Test 4 --- Compression

Competing theories are scored by:

-   explanatory scope
-   assumptions required
-   predictive power
-   falsifiability
-   simplicity

Prefer greater explanatory power with fewer assumptions.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Test 5 --- Mechanism

Every theory must answer:

What process generates the observation?

Reject theories without mechanisms.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Test 6 --- Failure Analysis

For every incorrect prediction record:

-   failed assumption
-   failed evidence
-   failed mechanism
-   failed representation
-   overlooked constraint

Improve the methodology itself.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Test 7 --- Search Allocation

Evaluate whether attention was spent on the highest-value unknowns.

Questions:

Did we investigate the largest anomaly?

Did we spend too much effort refining instead of testing?

Could a cheaper experiment have resolved uncertainty?

------------------------------------------------------------------------

## Test 8 --- Reality Drift

Every year:

Re-run the evidence ledger.

Remove outdated assumptions.

Update confidence.

Retire failed theories.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Success Metrics

The framework succeeds if it:

-   Reduces false positives.
-   Finds anomalies earlier.
-   Produces testable predictions.
-   Transfers across unrelated domains.
-   Improves after failures.
-   Helps discover mechanisms rather than narratives.

------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Long-Term Vision

This is not a prompt library.

It is a research operating system.

Its objective is not to answer questions.

Its objective is to improve how questions are investigated.

The ultimate benchmark is whether repeated use produces explanations
that are more predictive, more transferable, and more resilient than
conventional reasoning.
