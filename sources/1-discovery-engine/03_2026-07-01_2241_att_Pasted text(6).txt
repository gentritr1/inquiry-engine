The theory this document converges on, stated first: every major institution of civilization — firms, schools, courts, markets, professions, states — is a stored solution to two scarcities: scarce cognition (thinking was expensive because it only came bundled inside persons) and costly verification (you could not cheaply know whether a stranger was competent or honest). Machine intelligence dissolves the first scarcity and most of the second. What remains scarce is what intelligence cannot synthesize: energy and matter in place, human legitimacy and consent, credible commitment, and embodied time. Civilization over the next half-century reorganizes around those residual scarcities — and the reorganization happens at one specific frontier: the boundary where machine-speed action meets human-speed legitimacy. Everything below is the derivation, the attack on it, and the honest residue.

Phase I — The Hidden Assumptions
Modern civilization's load-bearing assumptions, ranked by how much collapses if they fail:

Cognition comes only bundled inside persons. You cannot buy thinking without hiring a human. Every institution downstream of this — employment, education, professions, management, universities — is a machine for locating, ripening, renting, and organizing person-bound minds.
Verification is expensive. You cannot cheaply check competence, honesty, or facts, so civilization built proxies: credentials, brands, audits, licenses, reputation, bureaucratic procedure. Most of what looks like "trust infrastructure" is actually economizing on verification cost.
Labor is the distribution system. Wages are not merely compensation; they are civilization's primary mechanism for distributing income, status, structure, and meaning. Tax systems, pensions, cities, and family formation all assume it.
Coordination costs bind organizational size and shape. Firms exist because using the market is expensive (Coase); states are large because collective action is hard; hierarchies exist because human attention spans of control are narrow.
Institutions run at human speed, and this is fast enough. Courts, legislatures, peer review, and diplomacy operate on months-to-decades clocks, matched to an economy whose actors also ran on human clocks.
Population grows. Growth economics, pension systems, military power, and housing markets all quietly assume more young people arrive each generation.
Expertise accumulates slowly in individuals and dies with them. Hence apprenticeship, seniority, professions, tenure — and civilization's chronic loss of tacit knowledge.
Physical manipulation requires human bodies. All of manual labor, and the wage floor beneath it.
Persuasion is bounded by human throughput. Propaganda, advertising, and lobbying were constrained by how much tailored persuasion humans could produce.
Records are sparse and reality is self-evidencing. Most events go unrecorded; what is recorded is presumed authentic because forgery was costly.
Identity is singular, biological, and verified by presence. One person, one body, one actor.
Energy is scarce and geographically concentrated. The geopolitics of the last century is largely this assumption wearing a uniform.
Assumptions that I judge will survive and should not be forced to fail: humans will still seek status, meaning, and each other (biology outlasts economics); matter and desirable location remain scarce; adversarial dynamics persist (any technology of defense arms the offense); legitimacy — the willingness of humans to accept an outcome — remains a human-granted resource; and thermodynamics does not negotiate.

Phase II — Which Assumptions Fail
Assumption 1 (cognition bundled in persons): failing now. Probability of substantial failure within 15 years: very high; confidence: high. Supporting evidence: frontier systems already perform graduate-level analysis at ~10⁻⁴ the cost of the humans they emulate, and the cost curve has fallen roughly 10× per year for equivalent capability. Opposing evidence: reliability at long-horizon autonomous work remains unproven; progress could plateau — this is the single largest uncertainty in this document and is carried honestly through Phase VIII.

Assumption 2 (verification expensive): fails asymmetrically — and this asymmetry is the pivot of the whole analysis. Verification of claims and competence becomes nearly free (AI can check anything). But verification of commitment — will this counterparty actually pay, show up, bear the loss — does not, because commitment is not information; it is capital plus enforceability. Probability: high; confidence: high. Knowing becomes free; promising stays expensive.

Assumption 3 (labor distributes income): fails slowly and politically, not economically. The economic basis erodes within 10–20 years; the institutional wage system persists long after, defended by law and identity. Probability of eventual failure: high; timing: the most politically contested transition of the century; confidence in timing: low.

Assumption 4 (coordination costs): failing. Agents collapse transaction costs → the Coasean logic that assembled firms runs in reverse. High probability, 10–25 years, medium-high confidence.

Assumption 5 (human-speed institutions suffice): fails the moment machine actors transact at volume. High probability, visible already in fraud, markets, and content moderation. High confidence.

Assumption 6 (population grows): already failed. This is the one non-AI failure and it is evidence, not forecast: fertility is below replacement in countries containing most of world GDP; East Asia and Southern Europe face working-age contraction of 30–50% by 2060. Nearly certain; near-total confidence. Its interaction with AI is underanalyzed everywhere: civilization is losing human actors at precisely the moment it gains machine actors — the two curves cross, and most of Phase IV follows from that crossing.

Assumption 8 (bodies required): failing on a lagged curve. Robotics follows AI by roughly a decade because atoms iterate slower than bits. Medium-high probability by 2040; medium confidence.

Assumptions 9, 10, 11 (bounded persuasion, sparse authentic records, singular identity): failing together, fast. Generation is free; therefore records no longer self-evidence, persuasion industrializes, and identity multiplies (one principal, thousands of agents). High probability, already underway; high confidence.

Assumption 12 (energy scarce): weakening, not failing. Solar and storage cost curves make energy cheap and manufacturable rather than extracted and geographic — but intelligence demand may consume the surplus. Medium confidence; energy becomes the price floor of cognition, tying the two scarcities together.

Surviving assumptions (7 partially — expertise stops dying with individuals, which quietly ends one of civilization's oldest losses) are held constant hereafter.

Phase III — The Structural Laws
Compressing the failures above into the fewest laws with the greatest explanatory reach:

Law 1 — Cognition becomes a manufactured input. Thinking is produced in factories (fabs, datacenters), priced per unit like energy, and no longer rationed by demographics. Consequence: every institution that exists to locate, train, rent, or organize person-bound minds loses its founding purpose and must find a different one.

Law 2 — Knowing becomes free; committing stays scarce. When analysis and generation cost nothing, information asymmetries collapse — and with them, every business and institution whose product was knowing something the other side didn't. Economic trust re-anchors from reputation-and-credentials to staked commitment and enforceable recourse, which require capital, law, and legitimacy — things intelligence cannot synthesize.

Law 3 — The unit of economic organization shrinks from the firm to the task; the actor population inverts. Collapsed coordination costs dissolve organizational boundaries while machine actors multiply and human actors (demographically) shrink. The economy's default participant stops being a person or a firm and becomes a delegated machine actor carrying someone's authority.

Law 4 — Institutional time constants become the binding constraint. Machine-speed action meets human-speed legitimacy. Every domain reorganizes around a new interface layer that translates between the two clocks — and most new institutions of the next fifty years live at exactly this interface.

Law 5 — Reality stops self-evidencing. When generating any artifact is free, no record is presumptively authentic. Civilization must, for the first time, actively manufacture its ground truth — provenance becomes infrastructure the way clean water became infrastructure once cities made contamination the default.

Five laws. Each failure in Phase II is a corollary of at least one; most future changes in Phase IV follow from combinations of two or three.

Phase IV — Civilization Reconstructed
Grouped for coherence; for each domain: what survives, dies, transforms, and what new institution the laws demand.

Organizations and work (Laws 1, 3). The firm survives as a liability and capital container — a bonded shell that holds authority, reputation, and risk — while its interior (management, coordination, most white-collar function) hollows out into orchestrated machine actors. Management as a mass profession disappears; ownership, taste, and accountability survive. Work transforms from supplying cognition to supplying judgment, wanting, and legitimacy: deciding what should be done and answering for it. The learn-earn-retire life arc dies with it (education's section below). New requirement: the delegation stack — machine-readable authority binding every acting agent to an accountable principal.

Markets, finance, property (Laws 2, 3, 4). Markets survive and intensify — they are the coordination technology that benefits from cheap cognition. What dies: alpha from information asymmetry, most financial intermediation-as-analysis, and the audit profession's sampling model (when checking everything is free, the audit becomes continuous and ambient). What transforms: finance's core product becomes commitment manufacturing — bonding, insuring, and guaranteeing machine actors at machine speed. Property survives but acquires a new class: authority itself becomes an asset — transferable, priceable delegation rights. New requirement: clearing and recourse networks for agent-to-agent transactions (the acceptance function), and a systemic-risk manager for cognitive infrastructure, because a civilization running on three foundation models has a monoculture problem — correlated failure becomes the new bank run.

Government and law (Laws 2, 4). The state survives — it is the ultimate commitment institution, the root of enforceability — and gains relative importance under Law 2. Legislation-as-text transforms into legislation-as-executable-policy; the interesting constitutional question of the century is which decisions must remain at human speed on purpose (deliberate slowness becomes a designed feature, not a defect). Courts bifurcate: machine-speed resolution for the billions of agent-scale disputes, human appellate legitimacy above it. What dies: bureaucratic procedure whose real function was rationing scarce administrative cognition — most of what a ministry does is Law 1's corollary. New requirement: a legitimacy interface — formal machinery for converting machine-produced outcomes into humanly-consented decisions (the descendant of voting, standing, and due process).

Education and science (Laws 1, 5). Education's economic function (manufacturing scarce cognition) dies; its surviving functions — formation of persons, socialization, credentialing of judgment and accountability rather than knowledge — were always there underneath and become the whole institution. Science transforms most profoundly: discovery decouples from the count of trained scientists (Law 1) and becomes recursive where experiments are computational; the bottleneck migrates decisively to validation in physical reality — wet labs, clinical trials, instruments. The prestige economy of publication collapses under free generation (Law 5); what replaces it is attested experimental provenance. New requirement: verification infrastructure for machine-generated science — the referee function industrialized.

Healthcare (Laws 1, 6-demographics). Diagnosis and treatment planning follow Law 1 into abundance; the binding constraints become regulatory time constants (Law 4) and physical care capacity — which demographic inversion makes the scarce resource of mid-century. The aged-care sector becomes the largest forced market for embodied machine labor. What survives untouched: the human need to be cared for by someone who can be responsible — care bifurcates into machine-delivered treatment and human-delivered accountability and presence.

Manufacturing, transport, energy, cities (Laws 1, 3, energy corollary). Manufacturing follows cognition into the "designed everywhere, made anywhere" pattern; the scarce inputs become energy, materials, and permitted land. Energy transitions from extracted to manufactured, dissolving a century of geography-based geopolitics (medium confidence). Cities survive — they are machines for human meaning and matching, not just labor pooling — but their economic rationale shifts from proximity-to-work toward proximity-to-people; the office district is the organ that atrophies.

Media, art, relationships, religion, identity (Laws 1, 5). Content becomes free; therefore provenance and relationship become the product — what is scarce is not the artifact but the attested human origin and the shared attention. Art bifurcates into ambient machine-generated abundance and a human-provenance premium sector (the handmade-goods pattern, but for everything symbolic). Religion and thick community plausibly strengthen — they are legitimacy-and-meaning institutions, made of exactly what Laws 1–5 cannot commoditize (low-to-medium confidence; marked as inference). Identity under Law 5 requires active infrastructure: proof-of-personhood and proof-of-authority become as basic as passports.

Security and military (Laws 3, 5, adversarial persistence). The offense-defense balance tilts toward whoever industrializes cognition first; mass mobilization decouples from population (the demographic-decline security problem partially solves itself, and small rich nations become plausible military powers — a genuinely destabilizing second-order effect). Deterrence logic survives; attribution becomes the hard problem when actors are cheap, deniable, and multiplicative — Law 5 applied to violence. The persuasion frontier (Law on industrialized influence) makes epistemic security a formal branch of national defense.

Phase V — The Missing Institutions
Institutions that do not exist, become near-inevitable under the laws, and cannot be absorbed by incumbents:

The actor registry (Laws 3, 5): the machinery binding every machine actor to an accountable principal with a scoped, verifiable grant of authority. The precedent is exact: the 19th century admitted a new actor class (the corporation) and had to invent registries, agency law, and limited liability around it. Existing identity systems cannot absorb this because they assume one actor per body.
Machine-speed recourse (Laws 2, 4): arbitration that resolves millions of small agent disputes in seconds, with human appellate legitimacy above it. Courts cannot absorb it — their legitimacy is constituted by their slowness; they will delegate downward instead.
The provenance utility (Law 5): civilization's manufactured ground truth — attestation of origin for records, media, measurements, and scientific results. Notaries and platforms cannot absorb it: notaries lack scale, platforms lack neutrality.
The cognitive-systemic-risk institution (Laws 1, 3): a central-bank analog for the failure modes of shared intelligence infrastructure — monoculture risk, correlated agent failure, cascading delegation. Financial central banks cannot absorb it; the asset they stabilize is different, but the logic (lender/validator of last resort, macroprudential oversight) transfers.
The distribution institution (Law 1 + labor's failure): the successor to the wage channel — sovereign-fund-like claims on cognitive and energy capital (compute dividends, capital-endowment schemes). Welfare systems cannot absorb it because they are means-tested patches on a wage world; this must be an ownership structure, not a transfer structure. Highest political uncertainty of any item in this document.
The legitimacy interface (Law 4): the profession and machinery of keeping selected decisions deliberately at human speed and converting machine outputs into consented outcomes. The descendant of due process, engineered on purpose.
Phase VI — The Universal Infrastructure Beneath Everything
Strip away applications and keep asking what layer is missing beneath the rest. The descent terminates at two layers:

The binding layer: the mapping from any machine action to an accountable human or capital structure — identity, authority, provenance of delegation, and recourse, as one substrate. Every institution in Phase V presupposes it. (This is where the previous analyses' "acceptance network" thesis sits; the civilizational frame confirms it as infrastructure rather than product, and locates it one layer deeper than any single network.)
The fact layer: attested reality — the sensor-rooted, tamper-evident record of what physically happened, without which Law 5 dissolves courts, science, journalism, and markets alike.
Beneath these lies only energy-and-compute, which is being built by visible market forces and is not missing. The binding layer and the fact layer are missing, are presupposed by everything above them, and have no natural incumbent owner. That is the bottom.

Phase VII — Derived Industries
These now fall out rather than being brainstormed: cognition utilities (metered intelligence, with utility-style regulation and price floors set by energy); the delegation-infrastructure industry (registries, authority protocols, acceptance networks, agent bonding and insurance — the financial layer of Law 2); the provenance industry (attestation hardware, verification networks, epistemic security services); machine-speed dispute resolution as a service; validation-constrained science industries (autonomous labs, trial acceleration — selling the bottleneck, not the ideas); the embodied-care industry forced into existence by demographic inversion; legitimacy services (auditing not whether systems work but whether humans consented); and post-labor asset management (structuring the population's claims on cognitive capital). Standards follow the same logic: the delegation grammar (the machine power-of-attorney), receipt and attestation schemas, and personhood credentials — all destined for commoditized foundations, all more valuable to author than to own.

Phase VIII — The Attack
The strongest opposing positions, stated at full strength:

The economic historian: every automation panic since the loom has been wrong; comparative advantage guarantees humans work even when machines dominate absolutely; new task categories always emerged. Weight: this is the strongest attack in the document. Partial rebuttal: previous automations substituted for specific human capacities while cognition remained the human monopoly that absorbed displaced labor; a substitute for cognition-in-general removes the refuge sector. But the historian's deeper point survives: Baumol dynamics mean the bottleneck sectors (care, legitimacy, physical presence, housing) absorb spending and can absorb workers. Resolution adopted: labor doesn't disappear; it migrates to the residual scarcities — which is what Phase IV already claims. The historian and this document disagree less than either expected, mainly about wage levels, which remain genuinely uncertain.

The diffusion realist: electricity took forty years to reorganize factories; institutions veto speed; every timeline here is too fast. Largely conceded — Phase IX's dates carry low confidence and its sequence carries high confidence. One counterweight: diffusion of software into cognitive work lacks the capital-replacement lag that slowed electrification; adoption through existing devices is historically anomalous in speed.

The capability skeptic: current systems remain unreliable at long-horizon autonomy; scaling may plateau; the entire edifice rests on Law 1 continuing. Fully acknowledged: Law 1 is the document's single point of failure. If capability plateaus at 2026 levels, this document describes a much smaller transformation confined to content, analysis, and short-horizon tasks. Explicit falsifier logged below.

The political scientist: states will freeze existing structures — licensing walls, mandatory human employment, protectionism — for decades; law can override economics for a very long time. Conceded as a rate modifier and regional differentiator (this predicts divergence: jurisdictions that admit machine actors early compound advantages, the Delaware-effect at civilizational scale) but not as a direction changer.

The trust theorist's attack on Law 2: perhaps commitment gets cheap too — cryptographic escrow, programmatic staking, reputation at scale — making trust abundant alongside knowledge. Partially absorbed: mechanized commitment still requires enforceability against humans and capital, which chains back to states and law; crypto's decade of attempting trustless commitment without legal grounding is treated in Phase IX as the instructive failed path. Law 2 survives but sharpened: what stays scarce is not commitment mechanics but commitment backed by legitimate coercion — which is why the state gains.

Revisions accepted from the attack: timelines widened; wage-level agnosticism adopted; Law 1 flagged as the keystone risk; regional divergence promoted from footnote to prediction.

Phase IX — The Simulation
(Evidence where marked; otherwise inference; speculation flagged.)

2026–2031 — the friction era. Cognitive abundance is real but institutionally undigested; the visible symptoms are entry-level white-collar compression (early evidence already exists), provenance crises (fraud, synthetic media in courts and markets — Law 5 arriving before its infrastructure), and the first agent-commerce rails from payment incumbents. Inflection point: the first large-scale agent-fraud or correlated-agent-failure incident, which does for the binding layer what 1907 did for the Federal Reserve — crisis precedes institution, reliably. Failed path of the era: attempts to solve provenance by detection (an arms race the generator wins) rather than by attestation at origin.

2031–2038 — the retrofit era. Robotics arrives at scale, forced first through aged care and logistics by demographic arithmetic (evidence-grade demography, inference-grade timing). Actor registries and know-your-agent rules harden after the crises of the prior era. Distribution politics turns acute as the wage channel visibly narrows; first sovereign compute-dividend experiments (speculation as to form, inference as to necessity). Regional divergence becomes measurable — the jurisdictions that built legitimacy interfaces early run government at a different clock speed. Bottleneck of the era: not intelligence but validation — trials, permits, physical testing; whole industries form around widening it.

2038–2050 — the inversion era. Machine actors outnumber human actors in economic transactions (the Phase III crossing). Firms are visibly liability shells around orchestrated capability. Science's rate limit is openly the physical-validation layer. The learn-earn-retire arc is a historical curiosity; formation-and-judgment education persists. Irreversible transitions crossed along the way: the actor-mix inversion (no polity re-prohibits delegation once its economy depends on it), the loss of presumptive record authenticity (Law 5 has no rollback), and whichever distribution settlement each polity reaches — ownership structures, once vested, are the stickiest institutions humans build. What survives regardless of which companies, parties, or nations win: the binding layer, the fact layer, the recourse machinery, and the legitimacy interface. Institutions outlive their founders' intentions; that is what makes them the true opportunity for anyone reading this at any point on the curve.

Phase X — The Deepest Compression
Two theories competed for the bottom layer:

T1 (cognition-first): civilization is organized around economizing scarce cognition; AI decompresses every institution built on that scarcity.
T2 (trust-first): cognition was never the binding constraint; coordination and trust were; AI is merely the latest coordination technology and the trust institutions absorb it.
Tested against Phase IV, T1 explains education, science, labor, and management but under-predicts why states gain; T2 explains the institutional persistence but not the collapse of the cognitive professions. They are not rivals; they are the two halves of the final compression:

Institutions are civilization's stored solutions to two costs: the cost of thinking and the cost of trusting strangers. Machine intelligence drives the first cost toward zero and splits the second — verification becomes free while commitment does not. Civilization therefore reorganizes around its residual scarcities — energy and matter, credible commitment backed by legitimate force, and human consent itself — and the new institutions form at exactly one place: the interface where machine-speed action must be translated into human-speed legitimacy.

Confidence: high that this identifies the correct axis of change; medium on all timelines; the theory's keystone dependency is continued capability progress (Law 1).

What would strengthen it: sustained divergence between falling cognitive-task prices and rising commitment/legitimacy-service prices; agent-transaction volume growth; the predicted crisis-then-institution sequence for the binding layer; care and validation sectors absorbing labor share.
What would weaken or kill it: capability plateau (kills Law 1 and most of the edifice); wage share holding steady through 2035 (vindicates the historian); commitment costs falling as fast as verification costs (kills Law 2's asymmetry); fertility recovery (removes the demographic forcing function).

The judgment this document should be held to: not whether it surprised anyone, but whether, decades from now, the institutions that actually formed — the registries, the recourse networks, the provenance utilities, the legitimacy interfaces — formed where it said the pressure was: at the boundary between what machines can do and what humans will accept.
